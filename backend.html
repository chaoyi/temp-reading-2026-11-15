        The rows come from another process. Where does a request spend its time, and what happens when I ask faster than it can answer?

        The table's rows now come from a backend over a WebSocket. The backend writes its own trace events on the clock Chrome uses, and the harness puts them into the same file: one trace, two programs, one time axis. This module follows a single request through three processes and six threads, then drags a scrollbar against a backend that serves one 40 ms query at a time and watches the line grow under three request policies.

        Question I holdWhat would a wrong clock look like in this trace, and does this trace look like that?

        Predict first

        Suppose the backend's clock ran 1 ms ahead of Chrome's. What changes in the numbers?

        Up grows, down shrinks. The round trip is measured by the page alone, start and end on one clock, so an offset cannot touch it.         Knowledge I am using
        Monotonic clock
        A clock that only counts forward from an arbitrary start (here: boot). Every process on one machine reads the same one. It says nothing about wall time and nothing about another machine.
        Causality as a test
        A message cannot arrive before it was sent. If the cross-process differences are positive and small for every message, the clocks agree to within that small amount.

        Network service

The server finished 50 of them in the 2 s, so about 29 are ahead of it. The check in a trace: waitedMs on successive query spans should climb by a constant.

        4 · Three policies For "every range", "one in flight, newest next" and "every range + cancel", say what bounds the wait for the final answer after the input stops, against a server that cannot be interrupted.
        Answer

        Every range: nothing bounds it; it is the whole backlog, (overload time) × (ρ − 1) plus one service. One in flight: the rest of the query in service plus one service time, at most 2 × S. Cancel: the same 2 × S bound (the query in service cannot be stopped), with a line of at most the excess load; the cancels saved 6 of 30 queries at ρ = 1.32, about 1 − 1/ρ.
